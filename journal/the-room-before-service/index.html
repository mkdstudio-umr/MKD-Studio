Journal 104

The Room Before Service

The Cipriani dining room sits with its back to Davies Street and waits. This is not the waiting of a room that has not decided what it is yet. It is the waiting of a room that decided long ago and is simply holding its position.

A restaurant before service is one of the more honest things a room can show you. Nobody is performing for a table. Nobody is leaning in to make a point. The linen is laid but undisturbed. The glasses are set but untouched. The portrait above the banquette watches an empty room. This is the purest state a dining room reaches: the argument assembled, nobody yet to receive it.

Service will change everything about the sound and almost nothing about the room. The chairs will be occupied. A silver pan will come out, and then another. But the round tables will still be round, the linen will still be pressed, the portrait will still be watching from the same angle. The guests are welcome, but they are not the point.

Most of what a good restaurant communicates happens in this window: after it is set and before anyone arrives. The chef knows this, which is why they walk the room before service. The photographer knows it too.

Journal 094

A Room Waiting for Someone

The sign is lit. The lantern is on. The stairs have been swept and the door stands open onto an evening that has not started yet. This is one of the most photographed hours in hospitality and one of the least examined: the room after it is ready and before anyone comes.

A place waiting for guests has a particular charge. Everything is at its best and nothing has happened. The napkins are unmarked, the chairs untouched, the whole evening still folded up and unused. It is a held breath, and a camera is drawn to it the way it is drawn to a stage in the minute before the actors walk on.

Photographed at this hour, a venue promises rather than reports. It does not show you a good night; it shows you the conditions for one. That is often the more persuasive image, because a room full of strangers is somebody else's evening, while an empty room lit and ready is still available to be yours.

There is a quiet tenderness in it, too. Somebody turned that sign on and does not yet know who will answer it. The room has been dressed for a guest it has not met.

Every welcome begins as a room waiting in the dark.

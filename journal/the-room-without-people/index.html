Journal 091

The Room Without People

Almost every photograph in this archive is of a room with nobody in it. A made bed. A laid table. A lobby holding its breath before the first guest of the day. It is the quiet convention of hospitality photography, so common that it has stopped looking like a choice at all.

But it is a choice, and a strange one when you say it plainly. These rooms exist for people. A bedroom is the most personal space a hotel builds, arranged around a body that will sleep, wake, dress and leave. To photograph it empty is to photograph the one condition it was never designed for.

The empty room is not lifeless, though. It is expectant. The pillows are set for a head that has not arrived, the light left on for someone still in the corridor. A room without people is a room addressed to a single person, and the photograph works because the viewer quietly casts themselves in the part. The absence is not a lack. It is an invitation.

Put a figure in the frame and the spell inverts. The room stops being yours and becomes theirs. You are no longer imagining the stay; you are watching someone else have it, and something of the promise leaks quietly out of the picture.

So the archive keeps its rooms empty on purpose. Not because nobody was there, but so that anyone might be.

Journal 092

Who Is Missing From the Frame

Two shopping bags stand on the floor of a boutique, set down as if for a moment. A mirror leans against the wall and reflects no one. Nothing in the picture moves, and yet the whole scene is arranged around a person who is not in it.

Photographs of hospitality and retail are full of these absences. The cup with lipstick on its rim. The coat over the chair. The bag put down while its owner steps away. They are portraits with the sitter removed, and they often say more about the sitter than a portrait would.

An object left behind carries the shape of whoever left it. The bags belong to someone who chose Guerlain, who is somewhere in the room deciding on a second thing. You do not need to see them to know a great deal. The frame withholds the figure and hands you the evidence in its place.

This is closer to how memory actually works than a face ever is. We rarely remember the people in a room. We remember the half-finished drink, the shoes by the door, the small proof that someone was here and has only just gone.

The missing figure is the loudest thing in the picture.

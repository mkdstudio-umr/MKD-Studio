Journal 093

The Staff a Photograph Keeps Out

The loungers are squared to the water. The umbrellas are furled at the same angle. The path is lit, the towels are folded, and it is late enough that no guest is here to use any of it. Everything in the photograph is the result of work, and none of the people who did the work are in it.

Hospitality photography almost never shows the people who make hospitality. The housekeeper, the groundsman, the porter who carried the loungers into line at dusk: they are kept out of frame as carefully as the cables and the fire doors. The finished image is meant to look effortless, and effort is precisely what it conceals.

There is a small dishonesty in this, and also an honest answer to it. The scene withholds the labour, yes. But it is a true record of intention. Someone decided the loungers should sit exactly like that, and someone else made them obey. The photograph keeps the decision even as it hides the hands.

The best of these images carry a faint unease, the sense of a place just vacated by the people who prepared it. Look long enough at the perfect arrangement and you begin to feel the staff standing just past the edges, waiting for you to leave so they can begin again.

Nobody is in the picture. Everybody made it.

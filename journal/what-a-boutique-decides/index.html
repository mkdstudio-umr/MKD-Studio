Journal 106

What a Boutique Decides

Réalisation Par on South Audley Street has a photobooth with tiger-print curtains, a coat stand holding one black dress, art books on a stone plinth, a mushroom lamp, and Georgian windows onto a private garden. None of this is explained.

The boutique that explains itself has already conceded something. It is uncertain whether you will understand it, and the explanation is the hedge. The boutique that does not explain itself has decided you will either read the room or you will not, and either outcome is acceptable. These are two different theories of retail, and you can usually feel which one you are standing in within thirty seconds.

The photobooth is not there because Réalisation Par sells photobooths. It is there because someone decided the space needed something to look at, and this was the right thing. The logic is aesthetic, not commercial. The art books are not there to educate. The mushroom lamp is not chosen for its light output. Each object is a position, and the positions add up to an argument.

You understand the brand before you have handled a single garment.

That is the ambition of the serious boutique. Not to display the product but to build the world that the product belongs to, and let the visitor enter it at their own pace. The garments are the confirmation of something the room has already said.

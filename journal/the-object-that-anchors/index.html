Journal 107

The Object That Anchors

The Byredo installation on Bond Street is a single bottle of perfume surrounded by handwritten paper tags. The tags do not describe the perfume. They record what people chose to remember.

One object in a room can hold more weight than everything else in it combined. The portrait above the Cipriani banquette holds the dining room. Without it, you have a well-proportioned space with good linen. With it, you have a room with a point of view. The portrait was chosen. Placed at that height, cropped to that scale, slightly overexposed. Someone thought about each of those decisions, and the room became different as a result.

The skill is in identifying which object the room needs, then giving it enough space to do its work. A room can only have one anchor. A room with two has none. This is why the Byredo installation is surrounded by empty space rather than product, and why the tags are handwritten rather than printed, and why the bottle is singular rather than a range. The restraint is not minimalism. It is the acknowledgement that the object needs room to speak.

What anchors a room is almost never the most expensive thing in it.

It is the most decided thing.
